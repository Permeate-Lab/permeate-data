Method & data

A defined scope comes before a price

Permeate Lab publishes indicative planning references for professional services. The intervals help frame a proposal discussion; they are not a representative market distribution, a provider offer or a complete project budget. Contributor identities and engagement details are withheld.

The four base intervals use planning guidance whose underlying project locations and observation periods are unspecified. Selecting a ZIP provides verified area context. It does not convert these intervals into measured local fees.

Expanded scope is shown separately. A numerical scenario is published only where its inputs can be reconstructed. Unknown charges remain unpriced. The 100-unit illustration is multiplication of a per-unit interval, before any minimum, fixed fee or additional work. Service tier, sampling and credential details are left open wherever the underlying guidance does not establish them.

Engineering service fees are separate from reserve contributions and repair construction. Do not combine basic inspection advertising, infrastructure studies, limited testing or bundled work as equivalent services.

Project endpoints use nearest-$500 display increments and unit endpoints use nearest-$5 increments. Existing base endpoints already match those increments. No inflation factor or arbitrary range expansion has been applied.

What the geography means

ZIP areas are Census ZCTAs where available, an approximation of postal ZIP geography. Area reference coordinates do not identify a project. Official condominium inventory records describe registered stock, not current service demand or condition.

County totals include records whose site ZIP cannot be reliably allocated. ZIP counts cover only screened records; a zero does not establish that an area has no condominiums.

Public records contain no contributor names, property names, addresses, engagement identifiers or private source links.
